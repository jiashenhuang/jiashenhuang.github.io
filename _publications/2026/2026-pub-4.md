abstract: >-
  Public trust in generative artificial intelligence has become increasingly differentiated in China with the rise of domestic large language models. While existing research has primarily focused on technical performance and individual-level factors, less attention has been paid to the broader institutional contexts that shape AI trust. Drawing on Cognitive–Affective Trust Theory, this study examines how institutional trust influences user trust in domestic (DeepSeek) and global (ChatGPT) large language models. Based on a within-subject survey of 405 Chinese users, the findings show that higher institutional trust is associated with stronger affective trust in domestic AI models and more favorable cognitive evaluations. By contrast, this domestic trust advantage becomes substantially weaker among users with lower levels of institutional trust. These findings identify institutional trust as an important mechanism underlying public evaluations of AI systems and demonstrate how governance contexts shape trust in emerging communication technologies. By linking micro-level psychological judgments with macro-level institutional environments, this study contributes to research on human–AI communication and digital governance in contemporary China.
